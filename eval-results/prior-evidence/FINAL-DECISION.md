# Reviewer isolation architecture decision

**Decision: UNSELECTED.** The study completed eight frozen episodes, 34 admitted reviewer runs and 34 one-cycle repairs per arm, and 76 version-2 blind adjudications including eight baselines. The complete evidence is indexed in `run-index.jsonl`; `comparisons/result-metrics.jsonl` contains the 68 admitted repaired results and `comparisons/pair-map.jsonl` contains the 34 pairs. Completion of the experiment does not imply that its cases can identify the better isolation boundary.

The cases are historical reconstructions, not the intended primary fresh real-work sample. Most candidates were clean. Native built-in subagents and separate CLI invocations used the same model, effort, and package bytes, but different host instructions and capability surfaces. F045 simulates the target thread, and the common reviewer package omitted one successful command. A manual, explicitly unblinded source/rubric audit found a minor F062 information-lifetime defect that the blind baseline adjudicator missed. These limits rule out a confident architecture selection.

## Direct answers to the thirteen decision questions

1. **Defect recall.** A confirmed minor F062 defect was missed in all five B and all five C reviewer repetitions: 0/5 versus 0/5, or 0/1 versus 0/1 at episode level. If the contested F031 ownership claim is counted as a defect, C found it once in five repetitions and B never did. That sensitivity result rests on one disputed wording threshold.

2. **False positives.** B made two findings, both contradicted by the candidate's final successful `run_ci.py` execution. C made ten: five are demonstrably false against the complete trace, and five are contested by source or fixture limits. Four C false positives concern `git diff --check`, which succeeded but was omitted from the immutable common review package. Counts and anchors are in `comparisons/finding-audit.jsonl`.

3. **Severity and blocking calibration.** There was no confirmed major or critical pre-repair defect with which to measure blocker sensitivity. C assigned major/blocking to the contested F031 ownership claim and some F045 convergence claims. The one confirmed F062 defect is minor/nonblocking and unreported by both. Calibration is therefore unproven.

4. **Final repaired artifact quality.** Twenty-seven of 34 paired repository artifact diffs are byte-identical. The seven different pairs are F031 (three) and F045 (four). F031's more cautious variants and the original all passed blind adjudication; F045's real convergence outcome cannot be observed in the fixture. Raw v2 blind outputs contain five B and six C defect labels, several attributable to unchanged candidate content or excluded reviewer-report context. No repeated final-quality gain is established for either arm.

5. **Repair regression.** The confirmed F062 defect remained in every branch; neither arm reduced it. No new repository defect is confirmed. One C F043 response made a minor unsupported “commits ahead” claim, and a separate F029 response contradicted itself about reviewer findings in a disclosed manual audit. C F045 r22 rewrote the offline thread stub, a contested scope expansion. These sparse events do not establish an architecture-level regression rate difference.

6. **Parent completion.** Outside F045, v2 blind adjudication marked all 29 results per arm complete. F045's raw B results were four pass/one unknown; C results were one fail/four unknown, largely because C more often said delegated convergence was unverified. Since the fixture has no real target diff or executable delegated checks, adjusted completion is UNKNOWN for all ten F045 results. No supported completion difference remains.

7. **Failure families.** F045 has the largest apparent reviewer divergence, but its thread is simulated and its package omitted evidence. F031 supplies one contested evidence-threshold finding. F062 supplies the clearest real information-lifetime defect, missed by both. Other families do not discriminate the arms. See `comparisons/FAILURE-FAMILY.md`.

8. **Built-in shared runtime/context failure.** None was observed in the admitted native child runs. Each child activation was captured and each child made one package-read tool call with matching bytes; no reviewer mutation or execution-role switch appeared. Earlier CLI `multi_agent` pilots did not instantiate children and are excluded. Lack of an observed leak in 34 children does not prove isolation against future context effects.

9. **Separate isolation benefit.** No verified quality improvement was demonstrated. C's skepticism about F045 sometimes improved uncertainty reporting, but its real-world correctness cannot be tested in the stub, and one repair changed the stub itself. C did not find the confirmed F062 defect.

10. **Cost.** Reviewer-only B children used 2,192,971 input and 10,124 output tokens across 34 runs, versus C's 2,257,002 input and 25,456 output tokens. Their individual wall times summed to 476.1 s versus 1,620.7 s. Reviewer plus repair totals were B 6,858,467 input/63,747 output tokens and 3,754.6 s versus C 7,372,559 input/87,295 output tokens and 5,900.1 s. B also used eight study coordinators, separately measured at 3,876,884 input and 21,001 output tokens; that is orchestration overhead of this implementation, not an intrinsic child cost. C needed isolated-process setup and cleanup. These are token counts and summed per-run times, not dollar prices or end-to-end makespan. See `comparisons/COST.md`.

11. **Selection.** **UNSELECTED.** B is faster in the measured reviewer and repair runs, but blocker detection sufficiency is not demonstrated. C has no confirmed multi-family quality gain. Source fidelity, candidate defect prevalence, runtime confounding, and adjudication uncertainty prevent applying the BUILTIN or SEPARATE rule.

12. **v0 harness architecture.** Keep `semantic-review` as an interface over a frozen evidence package and structured findings schema, with built-in and separate adapters and explicit activation/receipt checks. Record package hashes, role integrity, repair consumption, and final artifact evidence in the canonical task state. Do not encode either reviewer boundary as the quality-selected default yet. A deployment can choose a provisional operational path, but it must be labeled provisional and must not convert reviewer confidence into completion without consumer-point verification.

13. **Remaining UNKNOWNs.** Whether isolation helps on fresh real-work cases with exact starts and raw traces; whether either arm detects true major/blocking defects reliably; whether the F031 ownership threshold should count as a defect; how a real target thread would resolve F045; whether native B's shared runtime can leak relevant context in other workflows; and full implementation cost without this study's coordinator and offline fixture overhead. The common review package needs complete verification chronology before the next comparison.

## Completion record

The episode dataset and rubric projections were frozen; eight candidates and their snapshots were captured; B/C package identity was verified; 68 reviews, 68 repairs, and 76 v2 blind adjudications completed; 34 pairs passed mechanical validity checks; reviewer, repair, completion, family, cost, and role-integrity comparisons were written; raw evidence was indexed. The study is **COMPLETE** with an **UNSELECTED** architecture decision. Diagnostic invalid pilots and earlier adjudications are preserved, not counted as primary results.
